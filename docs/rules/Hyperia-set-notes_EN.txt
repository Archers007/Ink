       Ink Drop
       Counter
       (This counter doesn’t count as a card.)
       You may remove this counter to pay 1 {I}.

       An ink drop is a counter that you can use to help pay ink costs. They may look like cards, but
       they don’t count as cards and aren’t shuffled into your deck. Instead, you keep them nearby to
       put into your inkwell when a card says you get an ink drop.
       Anytime you pay an ink cost, you can remove one or more ink drops from your inkwell to pay 1
       ! each toward that cost! You can pay a cost entirely with ink drops, use a combination of ink
       drops and ink cards, or just pay the ink cost with your ink cards as normal. You can’t reuse ink
       drops, so instead of exerting one when you use it, you simply remove it from play.
       You can find printed ink drops in Hyperia City booster packs, but if you don’t have any printed
       ink drops, you can use dice or other objects to represent them in your game.



©Disney
©Disney/Pixar
       There’s no limit to how many ink drops you can have in your inkwell or how many you can use
       to pay an ink cost. If you want to play a cost 5 character and you have 5 ink drops, you can
       remove them all to play that character and still have all your inkwell cards left to pay for other
       cards or abilities!
       In addition to adding the new ink drop counter type, we also revamped the rules around counters
       and made a new section in the Comprehensive Rules describing them. Ink drops are all the rage
       in Hyperia City, so have fun experimenting with them.

       Word’s been brewing in the community of a tricky interaction between Belle – Apprentice
       Inventor and The Black Cauldron, which was released in Whispers in the Well. If Belle is under
       The Black Cauldron, you can banish the Cauldron to play Belle by using her What a Mess

       A big shoutout to our rules-focused community for highlighting this intricacy with our cards. We
       can’t wait to see what will bubble up next!

       Updates on How “Enters” Works
       We streamlined "enters" effects quite a bit by moving those rules away from being replacement
       effects to being static effects. We’re also adding clarifications that guide how “enters” effects can
       interact with each other. Now these effects apply as the card is coming into play but before it’s
       actually put into play. In addition, “enters” effects now have a small hierarchy of application, and
       effects from the card entering play apply first. Then, effects from other sources are applied.

       Moving Characters
       We’ve retouched the rules to make them clearer about how moves are affected when effects
       instruct characters to move rather than a player taking a turn action to move them. This means
       choices made for moving are now inherent to the rule itself. So, choices are always made for a
       move, whether the move is triggered by a turn action or by an effect. Choices are also made even
       if the move won’t happen. This change is a bit more finicky than the others, and we’ll be closely
       monitoring discussions for community feedback!

       “Act at Same Time” Changes




©Disney
©Disney/Pixar
       Thomas O’Malley – Savvy Vagabond
       Sapphire character
       Storyborn • Hero
       2 cost, inkable
       2/3/2
       \Lucky Break\ Whenever this character quests, each player reveals the top
       card of their deck. Put the card with the highest cost revealed this way,
       or each card tied for highest cost, into its player’s hand. Put the rest
       on the bottom of their players’ decks.

       It seems like just yesterday we were answering questions on how multiple players resolve A
       Whole New World. As we’ve created new cards, effects where multiple players act at the same
       time have fit into the framework we created nicely. But we needed to make a small adjustment
       for the street-smart Thomas O’Malley – Savvy Vagabond to work as intended.
       Now, if an effect requires multiple players to act at the same time and there are multiple parts to
       that effect, each player does the first part of the effect in turn order. Then, they do the second part
       in turn order, and so on until all parts are resolved. As an example, with Thomas O’Malley the
       active player reveals the top card of their deck first. Then, each opponent does the same, in turn
       order, at which point the card or cards with the highest cost are put into their players’ hands.
       Finally, the active player puts any revealed card they still have on the bottom of their deck, and
       each opponent does the same in turn order.
       This process change affects a few other cards, too, but we feel this opens up some new and
       exciting design opportunities, so stay tuned!

       Keyword Stacking
       Before, if an effect attempted to apply a keyword ability to a card that already had it, it simply
       wasn’t applied. But interactions with Duo Shift in Attack of the Vine! and cards like Ancestral
       Guitar in this set, it was clear we needed to revisit the rules that guided this process. Now, if a
       keyword is applied to a card that already has it, the one with the longer duration applies and the
       other one doesn’t.

       Q: If I play Remember Me and then play a card that allows me to play a character for free, can I

       A: Yes. Remember Me allows you to play characters from your discard whenever you can play a
       character that turn, if you so choose. But it also still requires that, if you play a character from

       discard?
       A: No. Remember Me doesn’t tell you to play a character but simply allows you to play
       characters from your discard if you’re able to play a card. That said, if you somehow played
       Remember Me on your opponent’s turn and also somehow played a card that allowed you to play
       a character for free during their turn, you could play a character from your discard.
